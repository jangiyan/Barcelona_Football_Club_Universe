The Immortal Blaugrana

Spanning over half a century of unmatched artistry, tactical revolution, and pure devotion to the shirt.

JC
1973–78 Player · 1988–96 Manager

Johan Cruyff

Dutch 🇳🇱 1973–1978 (Player) & 1988–1996 (Manager) Forward / Manager

Hendrik Johannes Cruijff — known universally as Johan Cruyff — is arguably the single most important figure in FC Barcelona's history. Born in Amsterdam in 1947, Cruyff represented everything that Barça stood for: attacking, stylish football played with intelligence, creativity, and courage. He joined Barcelona as a player in 1973, breaking the world transfer record to do so, and in his very first season led the club to a La Liga title that included the famous 5–0 demolition of Real Madrid at the Bernabéu.

As a player, Cruyff won the Ballon d'Or three times and revolutionised football with the concept of Total Football — a system where every player could occupy any position, creating a fluid, attacking style that prioritised ball retention and intelligent movement. Cruyff returned to the club as head coach in 1988 and over eight seasons built the legendary Dream Team, introducing a 4-3-3 formation, a deep connection to the La Masia academy, and a philosophy that would shape the club for decades.

His greatest achievement as manager was the 1992 European Cup, won at Wembley. His principles directly influenced Pep Guardiola — one of his players — and through Guardiola, the entire world of football.

Major Honours

La Liga ×4 (Player) European Cup 1992 Copa del Rey La Liga ×4 (Manager) European Cup Winners' Cup
LM
2004–2021

Lionel Messi

Argentine 🇦🇷 2004–2021 Forward / Right Winger

Lionel Andrés Messi was born in Rosario, Argentina, in 1987. Diagnosed with a growth hormone deficiency as a child, FC Barcelona's youth academy agreed to fund his medical treatment in exchange for his signing at just 13 years old. It proved to be one of the greatest decisions in football history. Messi made his first-team debut in 2004 at the age of 16 and went on to spend 17 seasons at the club, becoming its all-time leading scorer with 672 goals in 778 appearances.

He won six Ballon d'Or awards while at Barcelona — four of them consecutively — and led the club to a remarkable collection of trophies. In the 2011–12 season alone, he scored 91 goals across all competitions, breaking Gerd Müller's long-standing single-calendar-year record. Perhaps most remarkably, Messi achieved all of this playing for a club that had nurtured him from boyhood. He was not bought as a superstar — he was made as one.

In 2021, amid Barcelona's financial crisis, his contract was not renewed despite his wish to remain. He left the club in tears, and an era ended. He remains, for many, the greatest footballer who ever lived.

Major Honours

La Liga ×10 Champions League ×4 Copa del Rey ×7 Club World Cup ×3 Ballon d'Or ×6
RO
2003–2008

Ronaldinho

Brazilian 🇧🇷 2003–2008 Attacking Midfielder / Forward

Ronaldo de Assis Moreira — Ronaldinho — arrived at FC Barcelona from Paris Saint-Germain in the summer of 2003. Over the next five seasons, he produced some of the most spectacular, joyful football ever seen at Camp Nou. His skill, audacity, and love of the game radiated from every touch of the ball. In the 2004–05 season, Ronaldinho was voted the best player on the planet, winning the FIFA World Player of the Year award and the Ballon d'Or.

His performance at Real Madrid's Bernabéu in November 2005 — where he scored two goals and was given a standing ovation by the rival supporters — is considered one of the greatest individual performances in La Liga history. Ronaldinho's era at Barça bridged two great periods: following the trophy drought that came after Louis van Gaal's tenure, and preceding Pep Guardiola's all-conquering team. He brought joy, relevance, and two La Liga titles, as well as the 2005–06 Champions League trophy. Crucially, he mentored a teenage Lionel Messi, helping the young Argentine adapt to first-team life.

Major Honours

La Liga ×2 Champions League ×1 Ballon d'Or FIFA World Player of the Year
XH
1998–2015 · Manager 2021–24

Xavi Hernández

Spanish 🇪🇸 1998–2015 (Player) · 2021–2024 (Manager) Central Midfielder

Xavier Hernández Creus was born in Terrassa, Catalonia, in 1980. He joined the La Masia academy at age 11 and went on to make 767 first-team appearances for the club — making him one of the greatest one-club players in the sport's history. As a central midfielder, Xavi was the master of possession football. His passing accuracy, spatial awareness, and ability to control the tempo of a match made him the perfect embodiment of the tiki-taka style that Barcelona and Spain's national team used to dominate world football.

He won the Champions League four times (2006, 2009, 2011, 2015) and eight La Liga titles with the club. As a deeply proud Catalan, Xavi represented everything the club stands for. After retiring as a player in 2015, he returned to manage the club from 2021 to 2024, guiding Barcelona through a period of financial rebuilding and nurturing a new generation of La Masia talent.

Major Honours

La Liga ×8 Champions League ×4 Copa del Rey ×4 Euro 2008 2010 World Cup Euro 2012

2002–2018

Andrés Iniesta

Spanish 🇪🇸 2002–2018 Attacking Midfielder

Andrés Iniesta Luján joined La Masia at age 12 and would go on to become one of the most elegant players in the history of football. Blessed with exceptional dribbling ability, close control, vision, and composure, Iniesta was instrumental in every major trophy won during Barcelona's golden decade. His ability to carry the ball through tight spaces, turn opponents in confined areas, and deliver precise final passes made him virtually unplayable on his best days.

Outside Barcelona, Iniesta is perhaps best remembered for scoring the winning goal in the 2010 FIFA World Cup final — a goal that gave Spain their first world title. But at Barcelona, his contributions were constant and irreplaceable across 16 seasons. He scored one of the most famous goals in Champions League history — an injury-time equaliser at Stamford Bridge against Chelsea in 2009 to send Barça to the final.

Major Honours

La Liga ×9 Champions League ×4 Copa del Rey ×6 World Cup 2010 Euro 2008 Euro 2012
CP
1999–2014

Carles Puyol

Spanish 🇪🇸 1999–2014 Centre-back / Captain

Carles Puyol i Saforcada was born in La Pobla de Segur, Catalonia, in 1978. A product of La Masia, he became the defining defender of Barcelona's golden era — and arguably the most passionate club captain in Barça's history. Known for his ferocious commitment, aerial ability, leadership, and willingness to sacrifice his body for the team, Puyol exemplified the values of FC Barcelona: loyalty, effort, and Catalan identity. He wore the captain's armband for nine seasons and never played for any other club.

Alongside Xavi and Iniesta, Puyol was part of the nucleus that won three Champions League trophies and numerous domestic honours. After retiring due to injury in 2014, he returned to the club in an executive capacity. His famous battle cry during pre-match warm-ups became legendary among Barça supporters, and his quote — "The stripes are garnet and blue, but they represent something far greater" — is considered one of the most meaningful statements in the club's history.

Major Honours

La Liga ×6 Champions League ×3 Copa del Rey ×2 World Cup 2010 Euro 2008
