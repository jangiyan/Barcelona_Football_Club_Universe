Camp Nou

The Home of Barça

Camp Nou (officially the Spotify Camp Nou due to sponsorship) is a football stadium located in Barcelona, Catalonia, Spain. Opened on 24 September 1957, it serves as the home ground of FC Barcelona. With a pre-renovation capacity of approximately 99,354 spectators, it was the largest football stadium in Europe and the third-largest in the world.

The name 'Camp Nou' means 'New Field' in Catalan, a language and culture the club has always championed. Over the decades, the stadium has witnessed some of the most iconic moments in football history: Johan Cruyff's debut, Ronaldinho's sublime skills, and countless Champions League nights under the floodlights.

Beyond its sporting significance, Camp Nou functions as a cultural landmark for Catalonia. The stadium has hosted concerts, political events, and public gatherings that go far beyond football.

Stadium History

A Stadium Through The Decades

From its groundbreaking architectural design in the 1950s to the revolutionary Espai Barça transformation.

1954

Construction Begins

The Catalan government approved plans for a new stadium to replace the ageing Les Corts ground, which had become too small for the rapidly growing fanbase. Construction began in 1954 under architects Francesc Mitjans Miró and Josep Soteras Mauri.

1957

Grand Opening

On 24 September 1957, Camp Nou was officially inaugurated with a friendly match against Warsaw's Legia. The stadium, with its distinctive oval bowl design, could hold over 90,000 spectators from day one.

1982

FIFA World Cup

Camp Nou hosted several matches during the 1982 FIFA World Cup in Spain, including a semi-final. The stadium's profile reached a global audience during this tournament.

1992

Olympic City

Barcelona's hosting of the Summer Olympic Games brought massive investment in the city's infrastructure. Camp Nou hosted the football final, and the city's newly built facilities have served Barça ever since.

1998

Capacity Expansion

Renovation work brought Camp Nou's capacity to its peak of 98,772 (later recalculated at 99,354). Roof improvements and safety upgrades followed UEFA requirements.

2010

El Clásico Records

Camp Nou regularly hosted record-breaking attendances during El Clásico fixtures. The atmosphere at these matches became globally recognised as among the most intense in world football.

2016

Spotify Camp Nou

FC Barcelona announced the Espai Barça project: a major renovation of the entire Camp Nou precinct, including a new roof over the stadium and modernised facilities.

2023–present

Espai Barça Renovation

The club began the comprehensive renovation of Camp Nou under the Espai Barça initiative. During the construction period, Barcelona played home matches at the Estadi Olímpic Lluís Companys. The renovated stadium is expected to have a capacity of approximately 105,000 when complete.

Stadium Life

More Than A Stadium

Camp Nou is a living museum of football.

FC Barcelona Museum

Located within the Camp Nou precinct, the FC Barcelona Museum is the most visited museum in Catalonia and one of the most popular football museums in the world. It houses trophies, memorabilia, and interactive exhibits.

The Spotify Camp Nou Experience

The Camp Nou Experience tour allows fans to explore the dressing rooms, walk the pitch perimeter, visit the press box, and see the historic trophies on display.

Espai Barça

The ambitious renovation project will transform Camp Nou into one of the most modern and spectacular football stadiums in the world, with state-of-the-art facilities and a roof that will enhance the famous atmosphere.

Note: Capacity figures, renovation details, and completion dates are based on publicly available information. Construction progress and reopening timelines are subject to change. Refer to FC Barcelona's official website for the latest updates on Espai Barça.
